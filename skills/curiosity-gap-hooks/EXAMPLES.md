# Curiosity Gap Hooks: bad vs good

Every example is illustrative. The numbers and names are made up, so swap in your own
real ones. Every BAD line names the test it fails (see section 7 of SKILL.md). Every GOOD
line names its gap shape and has a payoff the body can deliver.

The seven tests: Tooth (can the reader say what is missing?), Goldilocks (do they already
stand on that ground?), Pandora (is it ambiguous in a way they would act on?), Specific
shape (no promise-words), Hold (still open after line 3?), Guess (can they guess wrong?),
Payoff (does the body deliver it, truthfully?).

---

## LinkedIn

### 1. Vague list

BAD:
> 5 content lessons that will change your business 🚀 Read till the end!

Fails Tooth (lessons has no shape), Specific shape (promise-word "change your business"),
and Hold (nothing was opened, so there is nothing to hold).

GOOD (named-number gap):
> Three reasons a cold DM gets ignored.
>
> The first two are obvious.
>
> The third one is the one that cost me a month of replies.

Passes: the reader can name what is missing ("the third reason"). Payoff: the third
reason, in one sentence, then the story.

### 2. Topic with no hole

BAD:
> Here are my thoughts on why LinkedIn engagement matters for B2B.

Fails Tooth and Pandora. A topic is not a hole. Nothing is withheld.

GOOD (contradiction):
> A post got 400 comments and 9 buyers.
>
> Same week, a post with 30 comments brought 6 buyers.
>
> The big one had more reach. So why did the small one sell more?

Passes: the contradiction is the hole. Payoff: the small post spoke to the buyers' own
problem in their own words. The body must show that.

### 3. Wrong-guess bait

BAD:
> Why do most ads fail? Let's dig in.

Fails Goldilocks (generic), Guess (no obvious wrong answer named), and Pandora.

GOOD (wrong-guess bait):
> Ask most marketers why their ads stopped converting.
>
> They say the audience got tired.
>
> It wasn't the audience. Same people, same ads, different landing page sentence.

Passes: the obvious guess is named and marked wrong. Payoff: the sentence, shown with
before and after numbers, in the next lines.

### 4. Promise-word closer

BAD:
> You won't believe what happened when I rewrote one line.

Fails Specific shape and Payoff risk: "won't believe" is a promise-word with no shape.

GOOD (missing word):
> One word in my headline cost me a week of calls.
>
> It's not the word you think.

Passes: the reader knows one word is missing and that it is not the obvious one. Payoff:
name the word, show the swap, show the calls.

## Email

### 5. Generic subject

BAD:
> Subject: Our latest insights on buyer psychology
> hey {{ subscriber.first_name }}, hope you're well! This week I wanted to share some thoughts on...

Fails Goldilocks (no ground the reader stands on), Tooth (nothing missing), Hold (nothing
to hold). Also a pleasantry opener that buries the hole.

GOOD (contradiction):
> Subject: 28 to 84, same page
> hey {{ subscriber.first_name }},
>
> A bakery's landing page scored 28 out of 100 on the buyer check.
>
> Sales were fine. The owner nearly didn't touch it.
>
> Six months later the same page scored 84 and sales doubled.
>
> Guess before you scroll.

Passes: the subject names the shape ("28 to 84"). Payoff: the one section that changed,
in the reader's words.

### 6. Summary in the subject

BAD:
> Subject: Why your welcome email is costing you signups

Fails Tooth. The subject tells the reader the answer is the welcome email, which closes
the gap before they open it.

GOOD (named-number gap):
> Subject: The third line of your welcome email
> hey {{ subscriber.first_name }},
>
> Most welcome emails ask for two things in the first paragraph.
>
> The one that loses people is the third line. Here's the line.

Passes: the reader knows the hole is one line in a document they already have. Payoff:
that line, shown.

## Article intro

### 7. Table of contents in prose

BAD:
> In this article we will explore why buyer language matters for AI search and how
> businesses can adapt their content strategy accordingly.

Fails everything. It is a table of contents in prose. No hole, no hold, no guess.

GOOD (consequence gap):
> A founder sent 67 LinkedIn replies in 21 days.
>
> The pipeline at the end of the 21 days was £625K.
>
> They did not publish a single post in that time.
>
> So what were they doing all day?

Passes: the consequence is named and the cause is missing. Payoff H2: the answer, then
the method. The numbers in the article must match the numbers in the hook.

### 8. Fake suspense

BAD:
> Stay with me, because what I'm about to share will change how you think about SEO
> forever.

Fails Specific shape and Payoff. "Change how you think forever" is a promise with no
shape, and nothing is withheld that could be named.

GOOD (pre-announced hole):
> Somewhere in the next six paragraphs there is a number you will not want to leave
> alone.
>
> I'll tell you where it is.

Passes: the reader knows the hole is a number and that it is coming. Payoff: the number,
in paragraph six, with its source.

## Video cold open (first 3 seconds)

### 9. Topic announced

BAD:
> Hey guys, today I'm going to talk about why your landing page isn't converting.

Fails Tooth, Specific shape, Hold. "Why it isn't converting" is a topic.

GOOD (sealed container + pre-announced hole):
> [card held face down] I wrote the reason your page isn't converting on this before you
> clicked. It's one of three things. Most of you will guess the wrong one.

Passes: the card is a container, the three is a shape, the wrong guess is invited.
Payoff: turn the card over on screen. The card must say something specific enough to be
wrong.

### 10. Reopen missing

BAD (after the cold open above, nothing returns):
> ...so that's the reason. Thanks for watching.

Fails Hold. The container was never reopened, so the reader leaves with the itch
unresolved and no reason to stay.

GOOD (reopen at 50%):
> Still think it's the headline? Turn the card over again.
>
> Hold the card up. Here's the one on the card: the page answers the price question
> before it answers the question the buyer actually asked.

Passes: the callback is explicit. Payoff: the card's text, shown on screen, then the
evidence.

## Ad headline

### 11. Adjective soup

BAD:
> Grow your business with data-driven marketing

Fails Tooth, Goldilocks, and Pandora. Nothing is ambiguous, nothing to act on.

GOOD (missing word):
> Your buyers describe their problem in words that are not on your website.
> The report shows you which ones.

Passes: the reader knows their site and their buyers, and not the words. Payoff: the
landing page H1 must show a real mismatch above the fold. Only run it where the report
can deliver the words.

### 12. Superlative

BAD:
> The #1 AI copy diagnostic tool

Fails Specific shape (superlative is a promise-word) and Tooth.

GOOD (nearly-complete list):
> Every buyer asks four questions before they pay.
>
> Find out which two your page skips.

Passes: four is a shape, two is the hole. Payoff: the scan result. If the scan sometimes
finds one or three, write "which ones" instead. Literally true or not at all.

## Landing page H1

### 13. Hope-based H1

BAD:
> Transform your marketing into a growth engine

Fails Tooth and Specific shape. "Transform" and "growth engine" are promise-words.

GOOD (contradiction):
> Your ads get clicks. Your buyers still leave.
>
> Here's the sentence they stop on.

Passes: the contradiction names the gap. Payoff: the stop sentence, shown on the page
with the fix.

## Outreach first line

### 14. About the sender

BAD:
> Hi Sam, I love what you're doing! I help coaches scale with AI-powered marketing systems
> and would love to...

Fails Tooth (nothing missing) and Goldilocks (it is about the sender, not Sam's ground).

GOOD (contradiction + guess invite):
> Sam, I ran your page through my diagnostic.
>
> Persuasion scored 4 out of 10. Clarity scored 2.
>
> The distance between those two numbers is one sentence. Want to see which?

Passes: the numbers are on Sam's own page. Payoff: the sentence and the fix in the next
message, with no pitch attached.

### 15. Flattery that closes nothing

BAD:
> Your content is amazing and I think we could help you grow even more!

Fails Tooth, Pandora, Hold. It is praise with no hole.

GOOD (wrong-guess bait):
> Sam, your page is ranking. The question is why the people who land on it leave.
>
> Most people say the price. Your page has no price on it at all.

Passes: the obvious guess is named and marked wrong. Payoff: the real reason, from the
page, in the reply.

## Hook that pays too late

### 16. Unpaid gap

BAD:
> The reason is in the comments. Link in the first comment.

Fails Payoff. The core answer moved out of the piece. This trains readers that your hooks
are junk.

GOOD:
> The reason is three words long. Here they are: "the price line."
>
> Here's why the price line is the problem, and where to move it.

Passes: the payoff is in the same piece, in the promised shape (three words, named).

## Quick self-check before you post

Ask these four questions of any opener you wrote:

1. Can the reader say what is missing in five words?
2. Would the answer survive replacing "this" with the actual thing?
3. Does the body contain that exact shape, in one sentence?
4. Is every number real and sourced, or marked `[VERIFY: number]`?

If any answer is no, fix it before it ships.
